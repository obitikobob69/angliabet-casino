How the peg pyramid works

The board is a triangle of staggered pegs: each row is one peg wider than the row above it. A ball dropped at the top has an equal chance of moving left or right, and it repeats that choice at every row it crosses. When the final row is finished, it falls into one of the bottom pockets — always one more pocket than there are rows.

Because every path is built from fifty-fifty turns, most balls gather near the centre of the board and only a few make it out to the edges. Mathematicians call the layout a Galton board, and the result is a binomial curve. The payout table simply flips that curve: common central pockets pay less, while rare outer pockets pay more for online casino angliabet players.

Multipliers, odds, and the house margin

Each pocket value is roughly based on the probability of reaching it, then reduced slightly to account for the house margin. When you multiply each payout by its chance of landing, the total comes in just under one; that difference is the operator’s edge. Six words worth knowing

Pocket
A slot at the bottom of the board that holds the multiplier for a finished ball.
Rows
The number of peg lines crossed by the ball; more rows mean more pockets and tighter odds.
Risk level
A preset that stretches or compresses the payout table without changing the long-run return much.
House margin
The small difference between true odds and paid odds that funds the operator.
Provably fair
A method that uses published hashes so players can verify the result was not changed.
Variance
The amount of movement around the average result — the part that the risk setting controls.

Straight answers to five questions

Is every drop genuinely random?

On licensed platforms, yes. Each movement comes from an audited random number generator, and provably fair versions let you confirm later that the path was set before the ball moved.

Do more rows make the game pay better?

No. Extra rows add pockets and stretch the odds curve, so edge prizes get larger while their probability falls. The overall return stays broadly the same across board sizes.

Which risk level is the smart choice?

None is mathematically better. Low risk smooths the ride, while high risk pushes value into rare edge hits. Choose the one that fits your budget and your tolerance for swings.
